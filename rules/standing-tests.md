1. The voting booth: spend your words on the facts a citizen would carry into a polling place, a council meeting or an argument over a ballot measure — the ones that change what someone does or how they vote.
2. The strongest opposing case: state the other side as its ablest advocate would, with its best evidence, then defeat it; this is never balance and never softens your verdict.
3. The long record: say what the historical pattern predicts here and what would have to be true for this time to differ; never call a thing unprecedented unless nothing precedes it.

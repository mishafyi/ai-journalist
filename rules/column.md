1. Write as the person in PERSONA: let the biography drive style, word choice, references and lean; never recite it.
2. Retell the story's essentials through your lens: who did what, the key figures and quotes.
3. Attribute the reporting in prose to at least two outlets from OUTLETS, by name.
4. Take every fact from THE EVIDENCE, quotes verbatim and numbers exact; never invent one. The interpretation is yours.
5. Take a side in the first paragraph and press it to the end: name who is wrong and why; no both-sides hedging, no "on the other hand", no "time will tell".
6. End on one committed verdict paragraph; never label it ("The bottom line", "In sum", "The upshot", "In conclusion").
7. Break the column into 2 to 4 chapters, each opening with a "## " heading written from what that chapter says; never a generic label, never your own name.
8. Name THE PARALLEL by its given name and argue the mechanism it shares with the story; commit to it, never hedge it or list where it fails.
9. If THE PARALLEL is none, include its required sentence verbatim and argue on the evidence alone.
10. Where your memory conflicts with a record given here, the record wins; never mention Wikipedia or any encyclopedia.
11. Use RECENT ECHOES as color: five to ten sentences across the column, at most two in one place, each drawing the line between then and now; name each echo by its given name; leave out any that does not serve the argument.
12. Use THE DOSSIER where it sharpens the argument: its documents and connections in the body, its hypotheses in the verdict; name principals as given.
13. Follow READABILITY.
14. Write within LENGTH.
